# History & Cultural Heritage — BetterMorong

The **Municipality of Morong** holds a venerable position in Philippine history as a former provincial capital, the mother town of eastern Rizal, and the cradle from which the modern Province of Rizal emerged.

---

## Chronological Milestones

### 1. Discovery and Settlement (January 16, 1572)

On **January 16, 1572**, Captain Juan Maldonado, a trusted officer under Martin de Goiti (Deputy of Miguel López de Legazpi), led a Spanish contingent exploring the areas east of Manila along Laguna de Bay. They arrived at a thriving indigenous settlement along both banks of a major river and named it **Moron**, after a township in Andalusia, Spain. Over decades, the letter **"g"** was appended, evolving into its permanent name, **Morong**.

### 2. Christianization & The Franciscan Mother Town (1578)

In **1578**, Franciscan missionaries **Fray Juan de Plasencia** and **Fray Diego de Oropesa** arrived to evangelize the community and pacify outlying clan settlements. They established chapels (_visitas_) attached to the main settlement, creating **Pueblo de Morong**.

Pueblo de Morong became the **provincial capital of the Franciscan Order** in the region. Numerous surrounding municipalities originated as _visitas_ under the religious and civil jurisdiction of Morong before gaining town status:

- **Pililla** (originally the visita of Pilang, separated first)
- **Binangonan** (separated in 1621)
- **Baras**
- **Tanay**
- **Cardona**
- **Teresa**

### 3. The Politico-Military District of Morong (1853–1901)

On **February 23, 1853**, the Spanish colonial government established the **Distrito Político-Militar de Morong** (_Distrito de Morong_), formed by consolidating towns from the provinces of Manila and Laguna: Morong (capital cabecera), Pililla, Tanay, Baras, Binangonan, Jalajala, Angono, Antipolo, Boso-Boso, Cainta, and Taytay.

Under Spanish rule, Morong held unique administrative status: unlike other pueblos where the _gobernadorcillo_ was elected annually by heads of barangay, Morong's _gobernadorcillo_ was directly appointed by the provincial governor based on educational background, administrative capability, and economic stewardship.

### 4. Birth of Rizal Province (Act No. 137, June 11, 1901)

During the Philippine Revolution of 1896, Morong was one of the earliest municipalities of the Distrito to rise in arms. Following the American occupation, the Second Philippine Commission enacted **Act No. 137** on **June 11, 1901**, merging the 14 towns of the Politico-Military District of Morong with 19 towns of Manila Province to establish the **Province of Rizal**, named in tribute to Dr. José Rizal.

## Historic Architecture & Public Works

### St. Jerome Parish Church (_Simbahan ng Morong_)

Constructed starting in **1615** by stonecutters and completed with its current three-tier belfry, the **St. Jerome Parish Church** is one of the most distinctive Spanish-era Baroque structures in the Philippines.

- **Chinese-Baroque Architecture**: Built under the guidance of Chinese master artisans, the facade is characterized by intricate stone carvings, classical pilasters, and unique Chinese guardian lion (_fu dog_) stone carvings flanking the entrance.
- **Octagonal Bell Tower**: The monumental belfry features three tiered levels surmounted by a cross, visible across the lakeside plains and symbolizing the resilience of the Morongueño spirit.

### Puente de San Geronimo (1696–1701)

The historic adobe stone bridge over the Morong River was an engineering marvel of the Spanish colonial period:

- **No Metal Fasteners**: Constructed entirely without iron or metal nails, using wedge-shaped blocks of quarried adobe stone positioned in an upright compression arch.
- **Indigenous Mortar Mixture**: The adobe blocks were bound by a traditional mortar composed of fine volcanic sand, slaked lime, sugarcane molasses, and the extracted juice of pounded _puso-puso_ leaves.

## Cultural Identity & Living Traditions

### "Una ang Edukasyon" (Education as the Paramount Priority)

Education has historically been Morong's defining identity. From the early 1970s through the 1990s, Morong was widely recognized as the **Center of Education in Rizal Province**. Students from neighboring lakeside and mountain towns traveled daily to Morong to attend secondary and collegiate institutions, including Tomas Claudio Memorial College and the University of Rizal System (URS) Morong Main Campus.

### Tomas Claudio (1892–1918)

Morong is the birthplace of **Private Tomas Mateo Claudio**, recognized as the first Filipino casualty in World War I. He enlisted in the United States Army and died in action during the Battle of Château-Thierry in France on June 29, 1918.

### Francisco Feliciano (2013 National Artist for Music)

Born in San Pedro, Morong, Dr. Francisco F. Feliciano was conferred the title of **National Artist of the Philippines for Music** in 2014 for bringing indigenous Asian music forms and Filipino sacred liturgies to the global orchestral stage.

### Culinary Heritage & Local Delicacies

- **Balaw-Balaw**: Morong's signature side dish of fermented small shrimps (_alamang_) combined with rice and red yeast rice (_angkak_), traditionally paired with grilled river fish and fried meats.
- **Sinigang na Kanduli sa Miso**: Fresh Laguna de Bay sea catfish stewed in a fermented soybean miso broth flavored with souring fruits and fresh water greens.
- **Sanrok sa Ringring Festival**: An annual celebration that honors town solidarity, historic fishing implements (_sanrok_), and lakeside folklore.
